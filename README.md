## 1. Introduction: a translation problem

In bed, a man and a woman often speak different languages, even in silence. A man stimulates his partner the way he likes it himself: harder, faster, more directly. A woman can't always explain what she wants, and when she does, she uses words he can't *feel*. The result is a gap that research records as the **orgasm gap**: in heterosexual sex, men reach orgasm considerably more often than women.

This essay starts from a simple observation: **male and female genitals are the same anatomical blueprint, built out in different ways.** If that's true, part of a woman's experience can be made understandable to a man not through explanation, but through his own sensation. Not completely, not perfectly, but enough to change his approach.

## 2. A shared origin

Until around weeks 8–9 of fetal development, the external genitals of embryos of both sexes look the same. Under the influence of dihydrotestosterone, the **genital tubercle** develops into a penis; without it, into a clitoris. That is why nearly every structure in one organ has a counterpart in the other:

| Clitoris | Penis | What they share |
|---|---|---|
| Clitoral glans | Glans penis | Densest innervation; the main source of sharp sensation |
| Clitoral hood | Foreskin | The same fold of skin, gliding over the glans |
| Corpora cavernosa | Corpora cavernosa | Fill with blood during arousal: both have erections |
| Clitoral crura | Penile crura | Anchored to the pubic bones, wrapped by the ischiocavernosus muscle |
| Vestibular bulbs | Bulb and corpus spongiosum | Located near the base; give a "background" sense of fullness |
| Skene's glands | Prostate | Produce similar secretions, including PSA |
| Dorsal nerve of the clitoris | Dorsal nerve of the penis | Branches of the pudendal nerve |

Importantly, most of the clitoris is hidden inside the body. Including the crura and bulbs, it is roughly 9–11 cm long. The visible glans is just the tip of the iceberg, much as the visible part of the penis is only about two-thirds of the organ, with the root lying in the perineum.

## 3. Where the analogy works

Homology lets us "translate" several key lessons.

**Direct vs. indirect stimulation.** Many women dislike direct contact with the clitoral glans and prefer stimulation through the hood. A man who tries stimulating his bare glans without lubricant will quickly understand why: it's too harsh. Meanwhile, the foreskin moving over the glans is exactly the kind of indirect route women describe as pleasurable.

**Glans vs. grip.** When masturbating, a man grips the shaft, but the main pleasure comes not from the squeeze but from skin gliding over the glans, corona and frenulum. If a man tries stimulating *only* the glans and frenulum, without moving along the shaft, he'll feel arousal build differently: more slowly, through rhythm and consistency rather than force and speed. This is close to how many women describe clitoral stimulation.

**Diffuse pressure.** Pressing a palm against the vulva transmits pressure inward, to the body, crura and bulbs of the clitoris. The male counterpart is pressure on the perineum, where the root of the penis lies closest to the surface. The sensation isn't sharp but "background", amplifying the main one.

**Penetration.** About 18% of women reach orgasm from intercourse alone; roughly 37% need clitoral stimulation, and 36% say it makes their orgasms better (Herbenick et al., 2018). The analogy explains why: during penetration, the clitoral glans, the most sensitive part, often gets no direct contact, just as a man would feel little if only the root of his penis were stimulated.

## 4. Where the analogy breaks down

An honest essay should name the limits of its own argument.

- **Scale.** The clitoral glans has a nerve count comparable to the glans penis, but packed into a much smaller area. The same touch feels more intense to a woman. A man can't reproduce that on himself, only approximate it.
- **No counterpart to the vagina.** The combination of external and internal stimulation can't be fully reproduced in men. The closest analogue is prostate stimulation, but that is a different experience.
- **It's not only physiology.** Emotional state, a sense of safety, context and the hormonal cycle all affect arousal in both sexes; technique is only part of the picture.
- **Individuality.** The difference between two women is often greater than between the "average" woman and the "average" man. The biggest risk of the analogy is that a man decides he now "knows" and stops asking.

So the goal is not an instruction manual but an **empathy tool**: "try it on yourself to understand the logic", and then "ask her how *she* likes it".

## 5. Orgasm: more alike than it seems

During orgasm, the pelvic floor muscles of both sexes contract rhythmically at intervals of about 0.8 seconds, heart rate and breathing change similarly, and largely the same reward systems activate in the brain. In a classic study (Vance & Wagner, 1976), experts given written descriptions of orgasm with gender cues removed could not reliably tell whether a man or a woman had written them. Later work (Mah & Binik, 2002) reached a similar conclusion.

The real differences concern what happens *around* orgasm:

- **Ejaculation** in men has a clear threshold, the "point of no return". Yet orgasm and ejaculation are separate processes, and male orgasm can occur without ejaculation.
- **A refractory period** is typical for men; many women don't have one.
- **The path to orgasm** is more varied for women: clitoris alone, penetration, or a combination.

This is another argument for synergy: at the peak, partners are closer than they think; they mostly diverge on the way there.

## 6. From empathy to practice

What a man who has "felt" the logic of female stimulation can actually change:

1. **Less force, more rhythm.** Steady, predictable movement often matters more than intensity.
2. **Lubrication.** Without it, stimulating a sensitive glans is unpleasant for either sex.
3. **An indirect approach.** Start through the hood, from the side, with circular movements, with the palm.
4. **Extra clitoral stimulation during penetration**: by hand, with a vibrator, or with positions that bring the pubic bones into contact (woman on top, the coital alignment technique).
5. **Feedback.** Ask, listen, adjust. No analogy replaces this.

## 7. A device as a translation tool

Part of this experience can already be had with existing devices:

- **Air-pulse clitoral stimulators** create pulsing air pressure around the glans; some men try them on the frenulum.
- **Wand vibrators** on the perineum and base of the penis give a diffuse sensation close to palm pressure on the vulva.
- **Synchronized toys** transmit rhythm between partners' devices through an app.

The product closest to the idea of this essay is the **Pulse** line by British company Hot Octopuss (since 2013). Its PulsePlate technology produces high-amplitude oscillations rather than ordinary vibration, stimulating the frenulum and glans without gripping the shaft and without requiring a full erection. Its roots are medical: penile vibratory stimulation has long been used for men with spinal cord injuries. The Duo version for couples adds a vibrating underside that stimulates the partner's vulva, with its own remote.

In other words, Pulse already makes the male experience resemble the female one: focused, targeted stimulation of the most sensitive zone instead of friction. But it was built for pleasure, not for *understanding*. That is where there's room for improvement.

## 8. The "Mirror" concept: proposed improvements

**"Mirror"** is the working name for a device concept combining pulsing glans stimulation with learning modes and partner synchronization. It is not a finished product but an open specification for discussion.

### 8.1. Interchangeable "hood" sleeve

A removable soft silicone layer between the pulsing plate and the glans, imitating the clitoral hood. Two or three sleeves of different thickness and firmness make it possible to compare:

- **direct** stimulation (no sleeve);
- **indirect**, through a thin sleeve;
- **diffuse**, through a thick one.

*Purpose:* to feel why many women prefer indirect touch.

### 8.2. "Scaling" mode

Interchangeable contact pads of different diameters: from wide (~25 mm, as in typical devices) to narrow (~8–10 mm). A smaller contact area at the same energy gives a more focused sensation, closer to how pinpoint touch registers on the small, densely innervated clitoral glans.

### 8.3. Perineum module

A separate or extendable vibration module at the base, resting against the perineum. It imitates the diffuse pressure that, when the vulva is pressed, reaches the internal parts of the clitoris. Its modes should be independent of the main plate.

### 8.4. Pressure sensor and smart response

A strain gauge in the plate that measures how hard it is pressed. Possible modes:

- **learning:** the device signals (with light or a rhythm change) when pressure exceeds a threshold, a reminder that "harder" doesn't mean "better";
- **adaptive:** intensity drops under excessive pressure, the way a partner's body would respond.

### 8.5. "Mirror" partner sync

The key feature of the concept. A pair of devices ("Mirror" for him, a compatible external clitoral stimulator for her):

- **Rhythm transfer.** She selects or creates a pattern on her device, and he feels the same rhythm on his in real time, at rescaled intensity.
- **Record and replay.** She records a pattern she likes, and he can "listen" to it on himself beforehand.
- **Control swap.** Each partner can control the other's device.

Sync should work via direct Bluetooth between devices, with no mandatory cloud service.

### 8.6. Learning app (optional)

- Short anatomical explainers with the homology table (as in section 2).
- Step-by-step "empathy exercises": compare direct vs. indirect stimulation, rhythm vs. intensity.
- Conversation prompts: questions that help discover a partner's preferences.
- The device must be fully usable without the app.

### 8.7. Biofeedback (experimental)

Optional heart-rate sensor pairing to display both partners' physiological arousal. It could help synchronize pace. The feature is experimental and privacy-sensitive (see section 9).

### 8.8. Baseline design requirements

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Contact materials | Medical/platinum-cure silicone only; no PVC, "jelly" plastics or phthalates |
| Design | Open, non-constricting; removable instantly |
| Protection | Waterproof (IPX7), sealed electronics |
| Safety | Auto-off after 20–30 min of continuous use, overheating protection |
| Power | Certified Li-ion battery, magnetic charging |
| Noise | < 50 dB |
| Accessibility | Works without erection, one-button control, support for limited dexterity |

### 8.9. For DIY prototyping

- 3D printing only for molds for silicone casting, never for body-contact surfaces.
- Ready-made certified vibration/linear motors and batteries; no mains-powered circuits.
- Before any body contact, check for sharp edges, overheating and seal integrity.

## 9. Ethics, safety, privacy

- **Consent.** Partner modes, especially controlling another person's device, activate only with the explicit consent of both; either partner can cut the connection instantly.
- **Privacy.** Intimate data (patterns, biometrics) stays local only, with end-to-end encryption for sync. No accounts by default, no selling of data. The market has seen "smart" sex toys quietly collect user data; that must not happen again.
- **No norms.** The device should not "score" orgasms or set expectations. Squirting, orgasm from penetration, multiple orgasms are variations, not standards.
- **Not a substitute for medicine.** Pain, sudden changes in ejaculation, or erectile problems are reasons to see a doctor.

## 10. Conclusion

Synergy between a man and a woman doesn't happen by itself, but it doesn't require miracles either. We have a huge advantage: our bodies are built on the same blueprint. Glans, hood, corpora, crura, bulbs: each of us carries a "dictionary" of the other sex's sensations, even if we've never opened it.

A device like "Mirror" won't teach anyone to love. But it can give a man what words can't: a bodily understanding of why "slower", "softer" and "a little to the side" aren't whims but anatomy. From there, the oldest and most reliable technology should take over: conversation.
